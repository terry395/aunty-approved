RED PACKETS (general Singapore custom, not company policy)
- In Singapore, unmarried visitors usually RECEIVE red packets from married relatives rather than give them.
- If you do give (for example, you are married), common amounts for kids are around S$8 to S$10. Use even numbers, never amounts with a 4, and new notes.
- Accept red packets with both hands, say thank you, and don't open them in front of the giver.

WHAT TO WEAR
- Smart casual and neat. Bright or red colours are lucky and welcome.
- Avoid all-black or all-white outfits (linked to mourning).
- Dress a bit modestly, since you're visiting the elders' home.

IF YOU FORGET SOMEONE'S TITLE
- Don't panic! Smile and say "Uncle" or "Aunty", then "新年快乐!" (xīn nián kuài lè).
- Quietly ask your partner later and use the right title next time. Elders appreciate the effort.

TEOCHEW FAMILIES
- Teochew titles are close to Hokkien but vary from family to family. Aunty gives the Mandarin title and suggests checking with your partner, or the Meet-the-Parents Kit's personalised cheat sheet, which is checked by hand.

GRAND-UNCLES AND GRAND-AUNTS (your partner's grandparents' siblings)
- Grandpa's older brother: 伯公 (bó gōng); Hokkien Pek Kong; Cantonese Pak Gung
- Grandpa's younger brother: 叔公 (shū gōng); Hokkien Chek Kong; Cantonese Suk Gung
- Grandpa's sister: 姑婆 (gū pó); Hokkien Koh Poh; Cantonese Gu Po
- Grandma's brother: 舅公 (jiù gōng); Hokkien Ku Kong; Cantonese Kau Gung
- Grandma's sister: 姨婆 (yí pó); Hokkien Ee Poh; Cantonese Yi Po
- Greet them like grandparents: "身体健康, 长命百岁!" (shēn tǐ jiàn kāng, cháng mìng bǎi suì).
